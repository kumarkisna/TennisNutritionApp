---
name: Evidence-based hydration guidance
description: Keep daily water references distinct from individualized sports hydration prescriptions.
---

Show National Academies Adequate Intake values as population references for total water from food and beverages, and label beverage amounts as survey estimates rather than personal targets. Activity logs alone do not justify adding a fixed daily fluid volume. A session sweat estimate may use pre/post body mass, fluid consumed, urine output, and duration, but must remain an approximate session measure rather than a prescription.

Hydration planning and logging must remain available on rest days. On tennis days, users can explicitly add hydration to a timed food slot; linked drinks remain in the daily hydration log as the single source of truth for planned and actual amounts.

**Why:** Age, body size, weather, acclimatization, exercise intensity, and individual sweat rates vary; a generic activity multiplier can mislead, especially for growing athletes.

**How to apply:** Preserve clear source attribution and disclaimers for hydration displays and exports. Keep drinks editable independently of tennis plans, including rest days; allow optional slot links on tennis days and unlink rather than delete drinks if their slot is removed. If future advice becomes prescriptive, require appropriate evidence and individualized inputs, and review with a qualified clinician or sports dietitian.
