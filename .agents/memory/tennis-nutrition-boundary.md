---
name: Tennis scheduling and nutrition targets
description: Why tennis schedules do not prescribe targets, silently retime diets, or own food deletion.
---

Treat tennis session schedules as timing context, not permission to automatically replace the player's daily nutrition reference or manual goals.

**Why:** Expected match durations can change, and session timing alone does not establish individual calorie, carbohydrate, fluid, or sodium requirements, especially for growing players. General tennis guidance must not be presented as a personalised prescription.

**How to apply:** Keep meal-timing guidance and workload information distinct from nutrition-target selection. Any future automatic workload-based targets need a separately scoped decision, verified sources, and clear age eligibility rather than assuming adult values apply to adolescents.

Treat scheduled food times as explicit user choices. Editing practice or match times must not silently move those diets. Removing a timetable slot or tennis day plan must not discard the food portions themselves.

**Why:** Tournament schedules can change without changing the player's intended food plan. Removing scheduling does not mean the user wants their foods or daily nutrition totals erased.

**How to apply:** Make retiming and actual food deletion explicit actions. Timetable and conventional meal views organise the same portions, not separate nutrition ledgers.
