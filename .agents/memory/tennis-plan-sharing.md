---
name: Tennis food plan sharing scope
description: The user's confirmed destination and date-selection scope for copying tennis food plans.
---

The user wants to copy tennis-plan-related food plans to “Another person on this device,” with “Date wise picking” allowed, and “same person on another date too.”

**Why:** The user explicitly confirmed local-person sharing and then expanded the scope to include reuse by the same person on another date.

**How to apply:** Offer independent source-person/date and destination-person/date choices, allowing the same person when the dates differ, even with only one saved profile. Treat people as local planner profiles, not online accounts. Cross-device sharing remains a separate request.

Copied portion amounts are reusable plans, not personalised nutrition prescriptions for the recipient.

**Why:** Different people can have different intake needs and reviewed goals. Sharing a food timetable does not establish that the source person's dietary amounts or goals suit the recipient.

**How to apply:** Preserve the recipient's personal references and goals; explain that copied amounts can be adjusted. Follow the separate tennis nutrition boundary when deciding what may change.

A 0–10 experience rating belongs to one person's whole tennis plan on one date. A copied plan does not carry the source date's rating, and an existing destination rating is preserved.

**Why:** The score describes the user's experience of the source-day plan, not the destination day or its nutrition suitability.

**How to apply:** Keep the score optional and per dated plan. Use it to filter the same person's local history without changing goals or nutrition references.

Food-timetable reuse copies timed slots and linked food portions, not the source's tennis sessions or notes. Destination activity and nutrition references stay unchanged even when the chosen day has no saved tennis plan.

**Why:** Importing an active session plan made food-only reuse on fresh or ordinary days require changing the destination activity. Food timing must remain independent of sports workload and personalised targets.

**How to apply:** Preserve existing destination sessions; allow a session-free food timetable when none exist. Do not silently add source training or matches to make a food copy valid.
